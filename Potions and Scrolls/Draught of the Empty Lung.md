The Draught of the Empty Lung is stored in a squat glass vial capped with a metal pressure seal. Around the neck of the bottle are dozens of tiny translucent spines that slowly extend and retract as though the vessel were breathing. A small button is set into the center of the cap. Until that button is pressed, the liquid inside is perfectly clear and completely still.

The draught was devised by an air primordial for mortal servants who had to capture enemies alive. Its creator found the usual mortal methods of restraint cumbersome. Chains could be broken, poison could kill the wrong creature, and physical force damaged prisoners who might still be useful. The primordial instead focused on the one weakness almost every terrestrial creature shared: they had to breathe.

The potion contains two separate substances. The clear liquid is an alchemical carrier, while the cap contains a minute quantity of compressed elemental air. Pressing the button punctures the internal seal and forces the elemental essence into the liquid. The protrusions along the bottle immediately extend, venting excess pressure with a faint hiss. The potion turns cloudy and must be consumed within 1 minute before the elemental air escapes completely.

The vial requires **attunement** before it can be activated. During attunement, the elemental essence inside adjusts itself to the wielder's breathing, pulse, and internal pressure. Anyone else who presses the button finds that the liquid simply erupts from the bottle as harmless mist. The restriction was intentional. The primordial did not want captured supplies being immediately turned against its own agents.

After drinking the activated draught, the creature feels no obvious change until it exhales deliberately toward another creature within 30 feet. The user can then release the stored elemental force as an action. The target must succeed on a Constitution saving throw or become **Stunned until the end of the user's next turn**. On a failure, the target does not suffocate in the ordinary sense. Instead, the air is violently pulled from its lungs while the pressure surrounding its body briefly prevents it from drawing another breath. The effect lasts only seconds, but long enough to leave most creatures helpless and disoriented.

Once the stored breath has been released, the potion's magic ends. If the drinker does not use it within 10 minutes, the elemental essence dissipates harmlessly through their breath.

The strange spines on the bottle are pressure vanes rather than decoration. They retract when the contents are stable and gradually extend as internal pressure rises. Experienced users learned to judge whether a potion was properly mixed simply by looking at how far the vanes had emerged. Bottles whose spines extend completely are considered unsafe and are normally discarded rather than opened.

The original primordial referred to the potion by a name that translated roughly as **The Reminder**. Mortal users gave it its current name after seeing prisoners desperately gasp once the effect released them. The primordial reportedly disliked the mortal name because, from its perspective, nothing had been emptied. The air had merely been somewhere else for a moment.
