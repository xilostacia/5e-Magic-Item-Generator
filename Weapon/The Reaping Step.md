The Reaping Step is a long-handled scythe made from black wood and a blade that seems to absorb light rather than reflect it. Its edge is thin enough to disappear when viewed straight on, and the weapon casts a shadow several feet longer than it should. That shadow never points directly toward the nearest light source. Instead, it leans toward the darkest nearby place.

The scythe was made by Fae of Shadenoct for hunters who pursued creatures capable of escaping through dense forests, ruined settlements, caves, and other places where ordinary pursuit became difficult. Its makers cared less about the weapon's ability to kill than its ability to ensure that once a chase began, distance alone could not end it.

While attuned to the Reaping Step, the wielder gains a special form of movement called **Shadow Passage**. As a bonus action, they can step into a patch of dim light or darkness within 5 feet and emerge from another patch of dim light or darkness they can see within 30 feet. The movement does not cross the intervening space. For an instant the wielder simply ceases to be where they were and emerges from another shadow.

The scythe's creators shaped the enchantment around the act of pursuit. When the wielder uses Shadow Passage immediately before making an attack with the scythe, the blade briefly becomes almost insubstantial. On a hit, the attack deals an additional **1d6 necrotic damage**. This is not a separate magical function from the movement. The weapon carries part of Shadenoct through the passage with its bearer, and that residue clings to the blade for a few seconds after they emerge.

The enchantment is double edged because every use draws the wielder farther into the same shadow they are exploiting. Each time Shadow Passage is used, the wielder's body becomes slightly less substantial until the start of their next turn. During that time, they have resistance to bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage from nonmagical attacks, but they also become vulnerable to radiant damage. Bright magical light interacts violently with the Shadenoct energy still clinging to them.

The weapon can awaken through continued use. A newly discovered Reaping Step can cross only short distances, and the transition through shadow is abrupt enough that many wielders describe it as falling sideways through freezing water. Once awakened, the scythe begins recognizing places of darkness around its bearer without conscious effort. Shadow Passage increases to 60 feet, and the wielder can use it even if they cannot see the destination, provided they know a suitable shadow exists there.

An exalted Reaping Step develops a far more dangerous relationship with darkness. The wielder can briefly pull another creature through the passage with them by striking it with the scythe and immediately stepping into shadow. The victim is dragged through Shadenoct for only an instant before being thrown out beside the wielder, but even that moment can leave lasting psychological effects. Survivors commonly report hearing voices, seeing figures moving beside them, or remembering places they have never visited.

The scythe's shadow theme is inseparable from its construction. Its handle is made from a tree grown entirely without direct sunlight, while the blade was quenched in water collected from a pool that had never reflected the sky. The Fae who made these weapons believed that shadow was not merely the absence of light. It was a place in its own right, thinly pressed against the ordinary world wherever illumination failed.

Very few Reaping Steps were made because their hunters had a reputation for becoming difficult to distinguish from the creatures they pursued. Older accounts describe veteran wielders who stopped using roads entirely, preferring to move from shadow to shadow even when no danger was present. Some eventually became uncomfortable in full daylight and slept only in windowless rooms.

The most troubling stories concern exalted weapons whose owners disappeared completely. Their bodies were never found, but the scythes were later recovered in places hundreds of miles away. In several cases, the weapon's shadow continued moving for a few seconds after the scythe itself had been picked up.

## Lore

The Reaping Step is a surviving example of Shadenoct Fae craftsmanship intended for pursuit rather than open battle. Its design reflects a view of shadow as an actual boundary space that can be entered and crossed, which explains both its movement ability and the dangers caused by repeated use.
