The Borrowed Face is a thin mask with no fixed appearance of its own. When lying unattended, it resembles a smooth oval of pale material with shallow openings for the eyes and mouth. The moment someone picks it up, its features begin to change. The shape of the cheekbones, nose, lips, skin tone, apparent age, and even the material from which the mask seems to be made shift according to the person holding it. Two people looking at the same mask in succession may insist they were shown entirely different objects.

The mask was created by Changelings, but not for Changelings. Its makers already possessed the ability the mask reproduces. Instead, it was intended for outsiders who had been accepted into Changeling communities, trusted intermediaries who needed to travel among them safely, and occasional agents who had to operate in situations where being visibly foreign would make their work impossible.

To its creators, changing one's appearance was never the important part. The important part was understanding that appearance and identity were not the same thing. The mask was therefore built to imitate the Changeling ability without allowing the wearer to treat it as a simple disguise kit.

A creature must attune to the Borrowed Face before its true magic functions. During attunement, the mask repeatedly changes while being worn. It cycles through versions of the wearer's own face at different ages, expressions, and slight variations, then begins producing faces the wearer does not recognize. By the end of the process, the mask settles into a form resembling the wearer's current appearance.

Once attuned, the wearer can speak the command word and alter their physical appearance in the same manner as a Changeling's natural transformation. They can change facial features, coloration, hair, apparent sex, height within reasonable limits, and other physical details while remaining the same basic body plan. Their equipment does not transform with them.

The transformation lasts until the wearer changes again, removes the mask, or ends the effect voluntarily. The mask itself becomes part of the chosen appearance and cannot be distinguished from the wearer's transformed face while the magic is active.

The command word has no direct translation. It is a Changeling phrase whose meaning depends on context, but the closest approximation is **"as I am needed."** This was deliberate. The makers considered a command like "change" or "become" inaccurate because the wearer was not becoming another person. They were only choosing another way to be seen.

The mask's tendency to change appearance depending on its wielder is tied to the attunement enchantment. It does not simply read physical characteristics. It reacts to self-perception. Someone who considers themselves older than they appear may see an older mask. Someone deeply uncomfortable with their appearance may see features subtly altered toward how they wish others perceived them. The effect is not reliable enough to reveal someone's innermost thoughts, but it has made the Borrowed Face an unexpectedly intimate object to handle.

The original masks were rarely sold. Most were given. Receiving one meant that a Changeling community trusted the recipient enough to grant them access to something normally inherent to their people. Losing one was considered embarrassing. Allowing one to fall into the hands of someone actively hostile to the community was considerably worse.

That is why many surviving Borrowed Faces have had their command words deliberately removed from written records. The object still works, but an unattuned owner who does not know the phrase may spend years believing they possess nothing more than an unusually reactive mask.

A few older masks are said to retain traces of previous wearers. While attuning, the new bearer may briefly see unfamiliar faces reflected in mirrors or feel the mask attempting transformations they did not choose. Whether these are magical memories or simply artifacts of repeated use is unknown.

Changelings who recognize an old Borrowed Face may care less about who currently owns it than about **whose face it remembers**.
