The Briarcall Gauntlets are a mismatched pair of supple green leather gloves reinforced with overlapping pieces of bark along the knuckles and forearms. Thin roots have grown through the leather rather than being stitched into it, and small leaves occasionally emerge from the seams before withering away a few hours later. They were made in Auriglade by a Fae hunting court that treated the hunt as a binding agreement rather than a sport. Once quarry was named, both hunter and hunted had obligations until the chase reached its conclusion.

While wearing the gauntlets, the bearer can speak their command word while naming a creature they can see or a creature whose trail they are currently following. A large Fae hound immediately emerges from nearby vegetation, soil, or another natural surface. The creature uses the statistics of a dire wolf, except that it is a Fey rather than a Beast. It understands the wielder's languages and instinctively knows the scent of the named quarry.

The hound remains for up to 1 hour. It obeys the wearer, but its purpose is specifically to pursue the named creature. It can follow tracks, search for the quarry, protect the wielder while pursuing it, and fight once it is found. The hound cannot be ordered to abandon the hunt for unrelated tasks. If the quarry dies, yields, or is physically captured, the hound bows to the wearer and dissolves into leaves and loose earth.

The gauntlets were originally worn by lesser members of an Auriglade court who were expected to pursue fugitives, oathbreakers, poachers, and creatures that had violated the court's territory. The summoned hound was not considered a servant. It was a witness to the hunt, there to ensure that the hunter pursued the quarry they had named instead of casually invoking the court's magic for some other purpose.

That is also the source of the curse.

Once the command word has been spoken, **the hunt must be completed**. If the hour ends while the named quarry remains alive, free, and uncaught, the Fae hound does not disappear. Instead, it turns toward the wearer. The wearer immediately becomes its new quarry, and the gauntlets tighten around their hands so that they cannot be removed by mundane means. The hound pursues them until it draws blood, at which point it disappears and the gauntlets loosen again.

Killing the summoned hound does not satisfy the curse. If destroyed while pursuing its wearer, it reforms at the next sunset and resumes the hunt. The only reliable alternatives are to allow it to wound the wearer or use magic capable of removing a curse.

This was not regarded as a defect by its makers. The Fae court considered it the necessary price of invoking another creature's participation in a hunt. Naming quarry without intending to finish the chase was itself a violation of the agreement. A hunter who broke that agreement simply exchanged places with the hunted.

Several mortal owners have learned this rule in particularly unfortunate ways because the gauntlets contain no written warning. The command word translates loosely as **"Attend the chase."** To someone unfamiliar with the court that created them, it sounds like nothing more than an activation phrase.

Older stories from Auriglade claim that the original court still recognizes these gauntlets. A bearer who repeatedly completes difficult hunts may occasionally find unfamiliar flowers growing from the bark plates afterward. A bearer who repeatedly becomes the hound's quarry may instead notice that the roots beneath the leather are becoming harder to cut.
