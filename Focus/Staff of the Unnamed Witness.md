The Staff of the Unnamed Witness is almost aggressively ordinary. It is a straight length of pale wood with no carving, ornament, metalwork, gemstone, or visible magical mark. Its surface has been sanded smooth, but not polished, and even its proportions are unremarkable. Unless someone already knows what it is, there is little reason to distinguish it from a walking stick cut by a competent carpenter.

That anonymity was intentional.

The staff was made by an aberrant intelligence whose exact nature has been lost. Surviving accounts disagree on whether its creator was a single creature, a colony of creatures acting together, or something that could only partially exist in the physical world. Whatever made it appears to have placed enormous value on observation while considering the act of being observed dangerous.

The staff was therefore built to attract as little attention as possible.

When an attuned wielder plants the lower end of the staff firmly against the ground and speaks its command word, the staff summons a creature known only as a **Witness**. The Witness appears within 10 feet of the wielder and remains for up to 1 hour, until dismissed, or until reduced to 0 hit points.

A Witness is a small aberrant servant whose appearance changes subtly from summoning to summoning. Some resemble pale eyeless animals, others look like bundles of folded limbs, and some are little more than floating masses of translucent tissue. What they share is an almost complete lack of aggression. A Witness avoids combat whenever possible and exists primarily to observe.

While a Witness is summoned, the wielder can use an action to see and hear through its senses until the start of their next turn. During that time, the wielder is blind and deaf with regard to their own surroundings. The Witness can be commanded mentally to move, hide, watch a particular creature, or investigate a location it can physically reach.

The command word is particularly strange because nobody has ever successfully translated it. Most creatures can pronounce it after hearing it once, but when asked to repeat it without holding the staff, they find that they cannot remember the sound. Written attempts to record the word produce different symbols each time.

The staff itself seems to consider the command word less a password and more an identification. When spoken correctly, something briefly recognizes the wielder as an authorized observer.

The staff can also awaken.

A newly discovered example summons only a single Witness at a time, and the creature it creates is simple, cautious, and limited in what it understands. As the staff is used to observe significant events, however, the wood begins changing in ways so subtle that they are easy to miss. Its grain develops patterns that resemble closed eyes. Small knots appear where none existed before. The staff remains completely plain from a distance, but close inspection reveals that its surface is gradually becoming more complicated.

Once awakened, the staff can maintain **two Witnesses simultaneously**. The wielder can perceive through either one and can mentally issue simple instructions to both. The Witnesses also begin behaving less like summoned animals and more like trained observers. They recognize individuals they have seen before, remember locations from previous summonings, and occasionally investigate things without being explicitly told to do so.

An exalted staff is considerably more unsettling. The few surviving descriptions claim that its Witnesses no longer vanish completely when dismissed. Instead, the creatures seem to go somewhere else. When summoned again, they remember what happened during previous manifestations and sometimes return with knowledge they could not have acquired while present.

This is where the staff's extreme plainness becomes more important.

Nothing about it advertises what it can do because its creator never intended anyone to know that observation was taking place. The staff was not a ceremonial focus or a symbol of authority. It was surveillance equipment. Its simplicity made it easy to carry into places where an obviously magical object would have been inspected, confiscated, or remembered.

Several historical accounts may contain references to the same staff without realizing it. Court records, military journals, and criminal investigations occasionally mention an unidentified traveler carrying a plain wooden staff shortly before information was leaked or supposedly secure conversations became known elsewhere.

The most disturbing possibility concerns the staff's awakening process. It clearly becomes stronger by witnessing important events, but nothing on the object explains why those events are valuable.

If the Witnesses are remembering what they see, then the question is not whether the staff is recording history.

The question is who eventually receives the record.
