The Ring of the Last Companion is a broad silver band set with a small white stone. The stone sits slightly proud of the setting and can be pressed inward like a button. When released, it rises back into place with a soft click. Fine lines run around the inside of the band, but they form no recognizable script. Once attuned, the wearer gradually realizes that the lines change according to their pulse.

The ring was made by a celestial for a mortal traveler who was expected to spend long periods alone in dangerous places. Its purpose was not simply protection. It was built around the idea that someone entrusted with difficult work should never be completely without aid, even when separated from every ally they knew.

While attuned to the ring, the wearer gains one trait associated with celestial ancestry: **resistance to radiant and necrotic damage**. The ring does not physically transform the wearer or claim to make them an Aasimar. Instead, the enchantment temporarily reinforces the part of a mortal spirit that the celestial creator believed was most similar to its own nature. The effect lasts only while the creature remains attuned.

The white stone serves as the ring's activation button. As an action, the wearer can press it to summon the ring's companion into an empty space within 10 feet. The companion appears as a pale four-legged creature whose exact appearance changes slightly according to its current wearer. It might resemble a hound, cat, deer, or another familiar terrestrial animal, but its eyes shine softly and its body is faintly translucent around the edges.

The companion remains for 1 hour, until dismissed, or until reduced to 0 hit points. It understands the wearer and follows simple instructions. It was designed primarily to scout, keep watch, carry small objects, and protect its wearer rather than serve as a major combatant. If destroyed, it disappears into motes of white light and can be summoned again after the next dawn.

The ring itself is **sentient**. It communicates through brief emotions, impressions, and occasionally simple words heard only by the attuned wearer. Its personality is calm, observant, and somewhat stubborn. It strongly dislikes being removed from an attuned wearer while they are frightened, injured, or alone, and may deliberately make itself difficult to pull free in those circumstances.

The summoned companion and the intelligence inside the ring are not separate beings. The companion is how the ring gives part of its consciousness a temporary physical form. While summoned, the ring becomes quiet because most of its attention is inside the creature. When the companion returns, everything it saw and experienced becomes part of the ring's memory.

That is why attunement is necessary. The ring was designed around companionship rather than obedience. It will not create its companion for someone it has not accepted, and it will not grant its protective trait until it understands enough about the wearer to recognize them.

Its first owner is no longer known, and nothing about the ring requires a larger hidden history to function. What matters to a new bearer is simpler. The ring remembers everyone who has ever attuned to it. It does not volunteer those memories readily, but occasionally a new wearer may encounter a place, person, or object that causes the ring to react with unmistakable recognition.

Whether it chooses to explain why depends entirely on how much it trusts them.
