The Ring of the Second Horizon is a narrow band of dull gray metal with no maker's mark, inscription, or recognizable style of construction. It appears old, but attempts to date it have produced wildly contradictory results. The metal shows no corrosion, yet microscopic examination reveals wear patterns suggesting centuries of use. Its strangest feature is its shadow. In ordinary light, the ring casts a thin circular shadow several inches larger than the object itself, and that shadow occasionally bends around nearby objects rather than falling across them.

No surviving culture claims the ring as its work. Its construction does not resemble known dwarven, elven, fae, celestial, fiendish, or elemental traditions. Scholars who have studied it generally agree only on one point: the ring was made by someone who understood perception very differently from modern peoples.

The Ring of the Second Horizon contains **5 charges** and regains **1d4 + 1 charges at dawn**. As a bonus action, the wearer can expend 1 charge to activate the ring for 10 minutes. While active, the wearer gains **blindsight out to 30 feet**.

The sense does not function like ordinary sight. The wearer becomes aware of the shape, position, movement, and approximate texture of everything within range, including creatures hidden by darkness or invisibility. Walls, furniture, weapons, and living creatures are perceived as complete forms rather than surfaces. A creature standing behind the wearer is just as clear as one standing directly in front of them.

The benefit comes with a deliberate drawback. While the ring is active, the wearer's normal vision is suppressed. They are **Blinded beyond the range of the ring's blindsight**. A distant tower disappears. Writing cannot be read unless it is close enough to touch. The sky, horizon, stars, and anything farther than 30 feet simply cease to exist from the wearer's perspective.

This limitation appears to be intentional rather than a flaw. Whoever created the ring seems to have considered ordinary vision distracting. The enchantment forces the wearer to abandon distant appearances and perceive only what is immediately real around them.

The unusual shadow is connected directly to that second sense. When the ring is activated, its oversized shadow spreads outward until it forms a faint dark circle with a radius of roughly 30 feet. It passes beneath walls and creatures without being blocked by them. To an outside observer, movement within that circle causes tiny ripples in the shadow, almost like disturbances across the surface of still water.

The wearer does not actually perceive through the shadow, but the two effects are expressions of the same enchantment. Whatever magic allows the ring to sense its surroundings also produces a physical outline of the area being perceived.

Ancient illustrations from unrelated ruins have occasionally been cited as possible evidence of the ring's origin. Several depict figures surrounded by dark circular fields while navigating structures with their eyes covered. The figures are never labeled, and none of the accompanying symbols have been translated. Similar images have been found in locations separated by enormous distances and belonging to cultures that should never have interacted.

One recurring image shows a figure standing before a doorway while wearing something on one hand. Beyond the doorway is a landscape filled with shapes that do not resemble anything known in Tennim.

The figure's eyes have been deliberately scratched out in every surviving example.

Whether the Ring of the Second Horizon was meant to help its wearer perceive what is nearby, or prevent them from seeing what lies farther away, remains uncertain.
